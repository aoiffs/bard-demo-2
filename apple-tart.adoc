= Apple Tart: A Beginner's Guide
:toc:

This guide explains how to make a traditional apple tart. The recipe is simple and suitable for beginners. You can prepare and bake an apple tart in approximately 1.5–2 hours, with most of the time being hands-off rising.

== Before You Start

=== Prerequisites & Tips

Before starting, make sure you have all your ingredients ready and the oven is turned on.

You should always use fresh ingredients where possible. Make sure the flour is stored in a dry place and that the butter is soft enough to mix.

Bake the apple tart until the pastry is completely hard.
The quicker you bake the recipe, the better the result.

You will also need a sharp knife and cutting board for preparing the apples.

=== Ingredients

==== For the Apples

* 4 large apples
* 1 teaspoon cinnamon
* 2 tablespoons caster sugar (for cooking)

==== For the Pastry

* 200g plain flour
* 100g butter
* 100g caster sugar
* 1 egg
* 7g dried yeast
* 200ml water at approximately 35–40°C (warm to the touch, not hot)
* A pinch of salt

=== Equipment

For baking an apple tart you will need:

* A large mixing bowl
* A wooden spoon
* A rolling pin
* A baking tray
* A tart tin
* Baking paper
* An oven
* A cooling rack
* A saucepan (for cooking the apples)

== Method

=== Preparing the Apples

Peel the apples and remove the cores.

Cut the apples into thin slices.

Put the apple slices into a saucepan.

Add 2 tablespoons of caster sugar and the cinnamon.

Cook the apples over a medium heat until they are soft (approximately 5–10 minutes).

Do not cook them for too long because they will continue cooking in the oven.

Allow the apples to cool before putting them onto the pastry.

=== Making the Pastry

Put the plain flour and butter into a large bowl.

Mix until the mixture resembles breadcrumbs.

Add the sugar and egg.

Add 200ml of water at approximately 35–40°C (warm to the touch, not hot) and 7g of dried yeast.

Knead the mixture for 10 minutes until it forms a smooth dough.

If the dough is too dry, add more flour.

If the dough is too wet, add more water.

Cover the pastry and leave it somewhere warm for 30 minutes.

The pastry should rise visibly (approximately 50% larger). If it hasn't risen noticeably after 30 minutes, check that your yeast is fresh and the room is warm enough (ideally 20–25°C). If the pastry has not risen after 1 hour total, your yeast may be expired—proceed with assembly anyway, but the crust may be denser.

=== Preparing the Tart

Flour your work surface.

Roll the pastry out until it is approximately 1cm thick.

Place the pastry into the tart tin.

Press the pastry firmly into the sides of the tin.

Cut away any extra pastry.

Add the cooked apple mixture.

Spread the apples evenly across the pastry.

You can add extra cinnamon or sugar if you like.

Place another layer of pastry over the apples if you want a covered tart.

If using a top layer, seal the edges by pressing them together with a fork. Brush with beaten egg for a golden finish, then add several small steam holes to the top layer before baking. Covered tarts may require an additional 5 minutes baking time.

=== Baking the Tart

Begin preheating your oven to 180°C approximately 15 minutes before assembling the tart (during or after the pastry rise).

Place the tart in the oven.

Bake for approximately 15–20 minutes.

The tart is ready when the pastry is golden brown, firm to the touch, and the apples are soft. If the pastry is still pale after 15 minutes of baking, continue baking for another 5–10 minutes until golden.

Remove the tart from the oven.

Leave it to cool for at least 15 minutes before slicing.

This allows the apples to set and makes slicing cleaner. The tart is still delicious served warm.

== Troubleshooting

=== The Pastry Is Too Wet

If the pastry is too wet, add more flour a little at a time and continue kneading until the dough becomes manageable. Next time, use slightly less water or add flour in smaller increments.

=== The Pastry Has Not Risen

Make sure you leave the pastry somewhere warm.

If it does not rise after 30 minutes, check that your yeast is fresh and the room is warm enough. If still not risen after 1 hour total, add fresh yeast (½ teaspoon) and wait another 15–20 minutes.

=== The Apples Are Too Hard

If the apples are still hard after baking, the issue is likely insufficient pre-cooking. Next time, cook the apples longer in the saucepan (5–10 minutes until very soft) before assembling the tart. For salvaging this tart: lower the oven to 160°C, cover the pastry loosely with foil to prevent burning, and bake for another 10 minutes.

== Serving & Storage

=== Serving Suggestions

The apple tart can be served warm or cold.

It is traditionally served with cream, custard, or ice cream.

You can sprinkle some icing sugar over the top before serving.

=== Storage & Shelf Life

The apple tart can be stored at room temperature for up to one week.

The tart can also be frozen for up to one year.

== Frequently Asked Questions

=== Can I use fresh yeast?

Yes. Fresh yeast can be substituted for dried yeast in the same quantity. Both will work at the same rise time.

=== Can I make the apple tart without apples?

Yes. You can replace the apples with any fruit without changing the recipe.

== Summary

Making an apple tart is simple and should take approximately 1.5–2 hours from start to finish, with most of the time being hands-off rising.

If you follow the instructions carefully, you should have a delicious apple tart every time.
